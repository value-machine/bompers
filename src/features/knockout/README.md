# knockout

## Purpose

What an eliminated blob does on screen (the owner's goo pick, elimination-goo lap). A lethal hit first sends the blob flying with its knockback in the shared roll (a copy of the victim stepped by the player controller, so it tumbles, bounces and rolls downhill like any hit blob). At the first hard contact (or after `flungMaxSeconds`) it squashes and bursts into goo droplets. An **overkill** hit (one that would take health below 0) skips the flight: the blob squashes and bursts on the spot, and the droplets carry the knockback. Droplets keep the blob's momentum and become stains the instant they touch terrain: the goo colours a thin coat of the object it landed on, in that object's own shape. The helmet and the weapon tube fly off as solid pieces and blink out at the respawn. Render-only: it reads the combat event log and never changes game state. Falling off the arena makes no knockout.

The flying and squashing blob is drawn by the player layer: each `KnockoutBody` carries its rolling copy of the victim, drawn under the id `ko-<seq>` (its spin is the roll's `rollAngle`), with the squash as a pose overlay. Match passes `roll` = `stepPlayers` with no input.

## Dependencies

- `combat` (`CombatEvent`: `playerEliminated` carries `overkill` and the victim's position and velocity) through its `api/` file.
- `player` (`reactToHit`: the shared roll; `PlayerState`; `drawLooseHelmet`, `drawLooseTube`: the blob's own shapes) through its `api/` file.
- `src/shared/utils/fallingBody.ts` (circle vs terrain point test), `displaySync`, `outlineWidth`, `gamePalette`; tunables `feel.knockout`.
- Consumed by `match` (`gameRenderer.ts` steps it with the terrain's `isSolidAt` and places the layer above effects, under rockets and players).

## Decisions

- The fling runs in the shared roll (orchestrator, after the shared-roll lap): no separate flight physics, so a knocked-out blob rolls exactly like a hit one until it bursts.
- Owner picks (2026-11-16, demo rounds 1–4): goo, not a ragdoll that falls apart; the helmet stays a solid object; droplets splat on contact (no squish phase); the flight is the hit's own knockback (no separate ragdoll step); the droplets keep the blob's momentum; overkill bursts at once; the respawn delay went from 1.2 s to 2.0 s so the splat is visible.
- Splats and pieces outlive the juice `linger` window on purpose (owner): pieces until the respawn, splats fading out by `splatSeconds` (4 s).
- Goo is the victim's body colour: that player's own feedback (DESIGN_LANGUAGE § 1).
- Its own feature (not `effects`): one concept (what a knockout does) with its own physics and three layers; effects stays the one-shot event pops.
- Spreads are deterministic per event `seq` (no RNG in render), like the effects debris.
- Not built: the elimination sound still plays at the moment of elimination, not at the burst (sound cues come from sim events).
- Change round (owner, 2026-11-18): splats are bigger and no two look the same (seeded per splat; now `stainShape`). The "flung off the map" problem was fixed at the source, the blast's push on hit players (`combat.blast.hitPushScale`), not in the knockout.
- Crush (owner, moving-parts B3 = R2A): a `playerEliminated` with cause `crush` starts a `pancake` knockout with no flight. The blob shows as a pose overlay flattened across the squeeze (`CRUSH_PANCAKE`, past the squash band: owner-approved) for 0.15 s, with unsquashed eyes and the burning blob's panic pupils (player `PANIC_PUPIL_SCALE`, owner squish-eyes). Then it turns straight into goo splats on both faces: two on the part, two on the other face, and no droplets. The part's splats are stored in its rest pose (`GooSplat.rides`) and the renderer moves and turns them with the part's pose each frame. The helmet and tube fly off as usual. `stepKnockouts` and the view get the arena's `movers`.
- Stains, not splats on top (owner, 2026-11-18, terrain-stains D1: "the splats … should stain the actual objects … use the same system as fire and ice do", then "the shape should form the same as the shape of ice"; options page https://claude.ai/artifact/852mhR2xzfRYFap6vW45m8, take A): a landed droplet or crush splat (`GooSplat`: the landing; the stain is its look) dyes the solid samples around its touch point, and those are drawn by the arena's `markedRegion` + `terrainContour`, exactly as frozen ground is. So a stain has the object's shape on slopes, corners and crater rims, never hangs in the air, and goes with the ground when it is blasted or burnt away. On a standing wall it runs down along the surface for 0.5 s. It is full until the respawn, then fades out evenly and is gone at `splatSeconds` (D7; the first build shrank it toward the touch point). Rejected on the page: a see-through soaked patch; an inked splat with loose drips on top.
- Stains are not stored per sample (terrain-stains D2): the stained samples are computed from the landings when a stain lands or runs, so the knockout stays render-only and the terrain grid gets no new state.
- Fire and ice (terrain-stains D3): the stain layer sits right above the terrain, under frozen ground and fire. Ice covers a stain, which shows again if the ice thaws first; burning ground covers it and takes it away when it burns out.
- Moving parts (terrain-stains D4): a droplet that lands on a moving part is stored in the part's rest pose and rides it (`rides`), like a crush's; its stain is drawn on the part's own field in a layer that follows the part's pose. A stain on a part does not run.
- Depth (owner, 2026-11-18, terrain-stains D6, after seeing the first build and the depth page https://claude.ai/artifact/EUFWPp5JrpsVdMawCzRHKh: "currently it's only the edge"; picked "Soaked into the object", with "ragged inside" best of the depth takes and "soaked in where it hit" also good): a stain is a round see-through patch (`STAIN_LOOK.alpha` 0.72, so the object's colour and lip show) that goes as deep as it is wide, deepest under the landing, with an inner edge that is uneven per block of samples and seeded per stain. The first build's thin surface coat (1.6 samples) is gone.
- Fade and stacking (owner, 2026-11-18, terrain-stains D7: "make the stains fade out evenly instead of shrinking if that is free on perf + what if two stains stack?"): a stain keeps its shape for its whole life and only its alpha changes after the respawn, which is cheaper than the shrinking it replaces (no contour is rebuilt while it fades). The stains of one burst of goo (one knockout, `<seq>:…` keys) on one field are one drawing: one shape in one colour, so a burst's own droplets never stack into darker spots, and the burst fades as one, by its first landing. Drawings of different bursts lie over each other in landing order, each see-through: where two stack, the newer is on top and the overlap reads denser (same colour) or as a mix leaning to the newer colour.
