# sound

## Purpose

Game audio: sound effects, music and the player's sound settings. After each frame the match hands sound a snapshot of the sim. Sound turns what changed into cues and plays them through Web Audio. Sound reads the sim and never writes to it. One audio engine lives for the whole app, so music carries from the lobby into a match and explosions duck it. The audio identity, palette and prompt template are in `documentation/game/DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` § 8.

Held loops (`readSoundLoops(world)` → `engine.setLoops`, every frame): `torch-loop` per player spraying a torch and `freeze-loop` per player spraying a freeze ray (pitched by the player), and one `fire-crackle` for the world while anything burns, gain by burning samples plus `fireBlobSamples` per burning blob up to `fireFullSamples`. A loop plays as passes of its file that overlap by `loopCrossfadeSeconds` with equal-power curves, scheduled a little ahead on each refresh (never `source.loop`: the mp3's silent edges would show as a seam; the files also carry a baked tail-over-head crossfade, DESIGN_LANGUAGE § 8). The engine fades a loop in and out over `loopFadeSeconds`; a loop the frame stops listing fades out, and one nobody refreshes for `loopHoldSeconds` fades by itself, so a paused or closed match goes quiet with no call of its own (Web Audio's last `stop()` wins). A round's end and the session's dispose clear them.

Music: the lobby screen plays `lobby`; a round plays `match`, then `final` from 0:30 left, and fades out when the round ends (the round-end SFX plays). Leaving a screen fades its music out.

Settings for other screens (the pause menu): `engine.updateSettings((s) => withChannelEnabled(s, "music", false))` switches music off without losing its volume; `useSoundSettings(engine)` reads them. The lobby's Music and Sounds rows step the volumes.

Hits and eliminations take the victim's pitch; actions (jump, fire, pickup) take the actor's pitch; explosions and round sounds are unpitched.

## Dependencies

- `features/combat` (event types) and `features/content-catalog` (sound IDs; the bank test checks every bank key is a catalog ID).
- Consumed by `match` (`services/matchSound.ts` builds the frame, plays cues and picks the round's music; `hooks/useGameSession.ts` drives it once per rendered frame) and `lobby` (lobby music, volume rows).

## Decisions

- Every sound ID is a content-catalog ID (kind `sound`), so levels and materials can name sounds. File paths stay in the bank because they are an engine detail, not level data (game-sound D1).
- Sounds that the sim does not emit as events come from the players' monotonic counters and phase changes, not from new sim events (game-sound D2).
- Spare takes the owner kept for later are in `public/audio/sfx/` but not in the bank: `dive-impact-spare-2…3` and `elimination-spare-1…2` (game-sound D3). Spares 1 and 4 became `crumble-2` and `clink-2` (material-sounds D5).
- Volume follows intensity (owner, 2026-11-16): landings by fall speed, hits by the victim's knockback speed, wall bonks by the speed into the wall; `SOUND_MIX.landingFullSpeed` and `hitFullSpeed` are the two knobs, the curve is `intensityGain` in `shared/config/gameJuice.ts` (material-sounds D6).
- Dive hits are quieter since the dive-push lap cut their push to 0.2 (≈ 210 units/s, gain ≈ 0.49, was ≈ 1000 and full volume); a point-blank blast hit is ≈ 600 (gain ≈ 0.74). `hitFullSpeed` stays 1000.
- A wall bonk's blob part is the landing splut, not `dive-impact` (owner's A/B, material-sounds D5).
- Soft jump and land (owner, soft-landing lap): `jump-1` and `land-1` are the original takes pitched down 3 semitones, low-passed and with a rounded attack, and deliberately quieter than the −16 LUFS norm (jump −21.4, land −25.4 LUFS). The quieter level lives in the files, so the mix code is unchanged (DESIGN_LANGUAGE § 8). The owner listened to 13 new ElevenLabs takes and kept this one.
- Re-trigger guard per body and sound (collision-sound-jitter): a player's own cue (landing, bounce, jump …) repeating within `SOUND_MIX.bodyRetriggerSeconds` (0.15 s, sim time) is dropped in `readSoundCues`; touchdowns under `SOUND_MIX.landingMinSpeed` (150 u/s) play no landing. The engine's `minRetriggerSeconds` stays the global pile-up cap per sound.
- The music bus exists before there is music, so explosion ducking is already wired (game-sound D5). Ducking now sits on its own node in front of the music bus, so it never fights the music volume (music D1).
- One audio engine for the app lifetime, not one per match: music carries across screens and both buses share one context (music D1).
- Sounds and Music volumes plus on/off switches per channel; M stays a master mute (music D2).
- The last 30 s of a round play a dedicated final piece, started in time with the clock (music D4).
- Music files are `lobby.mp3`, `match.mp3` (60 s loops, 32 bars at 128 BPM) and `final.mp3` (30 s, once) in `public/audio/music/`, one take each, processed as DESIGN_LANGUAGE § 8 describes; the bank test checks each track has its file.
- Events that name their own sound (`terrainDestroyed`, `partSound` from moving parts) play that ID. A crush (`playerEliminated`, cause `crush`) plays `land`; its `elimination` follows when the pancake bursts (owner, moving-parts B2; death-splat-sound lap).
