# player

## Purpose

The avatar: per-player simulation state, the controller system that moves players against the terrain, and the player render layer. Movement feel follows GDD § 2; the look follows `documentation/game/DESIGN_LANGUAGE.md` (Gumdrop Pop).

## Structure

| Layer    | Path                           | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |

| Types    | `types/player.types.ts`        | `PlayerState` — position is the **feet point** (bottom-center), y down; velocity, `grounded`, `facing`, unit aim vector, jump / coyote / buffer / wall timers, `wallDir`, `groundBelowY`, and monotonic event counters (`jumpCount`, `wallJumpCount`, `landCount`, `lastLandingSpeed`) for the render layer                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

Tests: `services/playerController.test.ts` (feel on a stone test level: accel ramp, gravity-scaled jump apex, jump cut, coyote, buffer, wall-slide, wall-jump, aim fallback, slope walking: crater exit, ramp speeds, leaving the ground), `services/roll.test.ts` (rolls down a hill, continuous strength, bounces, exits) and `services/blobPose.test.ts` (squash, helmet, foot motion on floor, slope and wall with the slope-to-wall handover, lean on slopes).

Seams for later work:

- **Tuning:** every movement and squash number is in the `player` section of `src/shared/config/gameTunables.ts`. Jump apex = `jumpSpeed² / (2 · world.gravity)`, so a match's gravity setting scales jump height.
- **Game feel:** the helmet (dome, gloss, emblem; its own ink) rides a damped spring (`HelmetWobble` in `blobPose.ts`, tunables `feel.helmet`), kicked by landings, jumps, wall-jumps, roll bounces and hits. A hit draws a white egg over the body fill (`hits` option, alpha from the effects feature's cues); the ink tree is untouched, so the silhouette never changes. Wall-jump rotation is not built yet.
- **Abilities (dive, later stuns / recoil):** write `steerScale`, `gravityScale` and `jumpLocked` on `PlayerState`; the controller applies them on the next step. Read `terrainHitX` / `terrainHitY` (+ speeds) to react to walls and floors. For a pose, pass `overlays` (`BlobPoseOverlay` by player id) to `syncPlayersView`; it combines with the spawn-protection blink and ring. An overlay may also raise the drawing (`lift`, a drawn hop; body and shadow stay where the sim has them) and shrink the pupils (`pupilScale`), both render only (the fire feature's panic look, fire-burn D1). Panic pupils share `PANIC_PUPIL_SCALE` (`blobPose.ts`): burning, and squeezed or crushed by a moving part. Every squash (land, hit, crouch, overlays) squishes the body but never the eyes: `placeEyes` keeps them at their spot on the squashed face and undoes the body's scale, so they keep their size and shape (owner, squish-eyes).
- **Squeeze (moving parts, owner #64 change round):** `PlayerState.squeeze` (`{ scale, nx, ny }` or null) is written by match from the movers ride each step. While squeezed the controller shrinks the body circle by `scale`, seats it on the feet and uses a 1×1 head box, so the blob can still walk and jump out of the gap; the renderer squashes the blob to match (render only).
- **Hurt reaction:** an attack applies its own knockback (`vx` / `vy`), then calls `reactToHit` with the impulse it gave; the blob rolls as hard as that impulse (0, like a burn tick, does nothing). Used by the dive's strike, combat's explosions (never on the shooter) and the fire feature's burn.
- **Planted feet (parked idea, not a task; owner, 2026-11-17):** the owner may want this later, so it is kept here and not on the backlog (nothing should auto-build it). Each foot plants on a real spot of the terrain and stays there while the body moves on, then steps ahead; each foot takes the angle of its own spot, so the front foot reaches a wall while the back foot is still on the slope; in the air they trail on springs. Needs the terrain passed into `syncPlayersView` from the match renderer, per-foot state, re-planting rules for blasted and moving terrain, and a cap so the feet don't read as a scurry at run speed (about 180 lines, 2–4 terrain look-ups per blob per frame). It would replace the step cycle in `blobPose.ts`, on top of the surface angle. Shown live as option C on the leg-locomotion options page: https://claude.ai/artifact/EyrrSWnufKSZdenNsYLGda
- **Combat** (`features/combat`) writes `vx` / `vy` for knockback and blast impulses (rocket jumps) and reads `aimX` / `aimY`; health, weapons and invulnerability live in combat state keyed by player `id`.

## Decisions

- **Body against terrain (slope-walking D7):** a round bottom (circle, radius width / 2) tested on the terrain distance field (`circleHitsSolid`), under a head box on the cells, inset half a sample at the sides and one sample above the circle's center, so cell steps never catch on a slope. Contacts back off by bisection (exact on flat ground). Airborne sideways moves into ground no steeper than the lip angle slide up it instead of stopping (A6).
- **Slope walking (slope-walking D1, D3, D8, A1–A3; supersedes terrain-curves D6's step-up):** while grounded the speed along the ground is (vx, vy) · tangent each step, so knockback and contacts keep working. Target speed on a slope θ: `runSpeed × (1 − uphillLoss · sin θ)` up and `× (1 + downhillGain · sin θ)` down (`player.slope`, 0.75 each: 30° up ≈ 62 %, 30° down ≈ 138 %), approached at the flat-ground accel / decel / turn rates (flat feel unchanged). On a slope, speed above the target bleeds at `carryDecel` (a run-up carries) and speed past runSpeed builds downhill at gravity × sin θ. No input: the ground decel, no pull, so a blob stands still on a slope. Each sub-step moves sideways and settles the circle onto the contour (`settleCircle`, rise and drop up to `|dx| · tan lipAngle`); a step that can't settle is a wall, or a **lip** when the body fits `lipRise` (12) higher: then it pulls up at `lipSpeed` (25 % run). Ground of `lipAngle` (80°) or steeper without a lip is slid off. **Leaving the ground:** velocity pointing out of it (a jump, knock-up, pop), no ground within reach (a ledge), or an edge sharper than `stickRadius` (56) taken faster than gravity can follow (speed² × curvature > g × cos θ) while not climbing; gentler curves always hold the feet. Landing keeps the velocity along the ground. `groundAngle` (radians, + rising right) feeds the lean.
- **One surface angle for the feet (leg-locomotion D1, owner pick; replaces the separate wall pose):** the feet have no ground pose and wall pose. One sprung angle (`feel.feet.surface`) says where the soles face: the slope under the blob (`groundAngle`), the wall it touches (`wallDir`, ±90°), or the air tilt; a rolling blob's feet ride the spin. Both feet sit side by side around the body circle's centre on that side, so they stay on the ground on steep slopes, and the step cycle runs on distance moved along the surface (equal to x on flat ground), so it carries on up a slope and on a wall. The root still leans by its share of the slope; the feet turn the rest (`rootLean`). The stacked wall brace and the wall-jump hold are gone.
- **Steadied contact for drawing (leg-locomotion D5):** where a slope is as steep as a wall (the lip angle, 80°) the controller slides the blob off and lands it again, so `grounded` and `wallDir` flip on every sim step while a player pushes into it. The render layer never draws those flags straight: `stepContactHold` keeps a ground or wall contact for `feel.feet.contactHoldSeconds` (0.1 s) after the sim stops reporting it, and a jump or wall-jump lets go at once. The feet, the slope lean and the contact shadow all read that steadied contact. The flip itself is sim behaviour and still there (it also counts a soft landing each time).
- Fixed take-off speed: apex scales with gravity, so low-gravity maps jump higher (player-movement D1).
- Tempo is set by gravity, height by speed² / gravity: default gravity is 1400 (was 2200) for more hang time, and every gravity-coupled vertical speed (jump, wall-jump Y, max fall, blast impulse, dive lift / pop) was scaled by √(1400 / 2200) so heights stayed. Retune those together (jumps-and-recoil D1).
- Variable jump = one cut of the rise on release (player-movement D2); linear accel toward the input speed with a turn boost (D3).
- Sim → render events are monotonic counters on `PlayerState`, never per-step flags, because a frame may run several sim steps (player-movement D4). Squash never feeds back into the sim.
- The animation clock is a `syncPlayersView` option defaulting to `performance.now()` (D5); the sim scans `groundBelowY` for the contact shadow (D6).
- Blob-vs-blob collision lives here (dive-headbutt D1): it is about `PlayerState` bodies and uses the controller's terrain-safe move. Bodies are circles for blob contact, boxes for terrain (D4). A blob that cannot give way is immovable for that contact, so others bounce off it (D5).
- Line style E is two passes of the same shapes (ink stroke tree under the fill tree), no filters, so a blob costs a handful of static Graphics; the spawn blink fades only the fill, keeping the silhouette readable (visual-pass D7). The blob's body top meets the collision height (`r = height / 2.3`), and the weapon tube is part of the silhouette, so it lives here, not in combat (visual-pass D8); its shape per weapon comes from the shared tube geometry (weapon-visuals D1).
- Ability systems steer the controller only through the generic motion modifiers; the controller has no ability-specific branch (dive-headbutt D2).
- Defensive crouch (owner feedback round 2 § 2.4): `PlayerInput.crouch` (keyboard down, d-pad down, move stick mostly down) crouches only while grounded, not rolling and not ability-steered (`updateCrouch`, sets `crouching`, counts `crouchCount`). A crouching blob brakes to a stop but still turns, aims, fires and jumps (a jump ends it); in the air down is only aim, so dive-down and rocket-down are unchanged (crouch D3, D4). The hitbox stays; the sink is render-only — `stepCrouchBlend` eases `BlobAnim.crouchBlend`, `blobPose` scales the body (head included) to `player.crouch.bodySx / bodySy` (crouch D5). The feet keep their normal pose: the owner overrode round 2's "feet almost invisible" (crouch D6). The crouched face (`crouchFace` → `BlobPose.face`): the helmet sinks `crouch.helmetDrop` u and the eyes rise `crouch.eyeLift` u and are re-ranked under the dome (torso fill draws by `zIndex`), so the rim covers about half of each eye; standing, the eyes stay over the dome (crouch D7). Combat halves the damage (combat README).
- The roll lives here, not in an attack feature (dive-headbutt D10): it is player motion state that several attacks and the dash trigger, and the ball physics and bounce have to happen inside the controller's terrain collision. Each roll bounce plays the landing squash.
- **One roll for every way into it (shared-roll D2, replaces the tumble):** hits (any attack through `reactToHit`) and the dive's dash-hold share one state and one physics. While rolling the controller skips the run target: on the ground speed along the contour gains `gravity × gravityScale × tangent.y` (rolls down hills, slows uphill) and loses `(rollingDecel + rollingDrag · |v|) × grip` (ice keeps it going); input only pushes toward run speed (`steerAccel`, × `lockSteerScale` while locked), never brakes a faster roll; in the air it flies with gravity. Terrain impacts at `minBounceSpeed` or more bounce by `restitution`, keeping `surfaceSpeedKeep` along the surface; no bounce count, so they die out by themselves (D4).
- **Roll strength is continuous (shared-roll D3):** the hit's impulse sets the lock (`|impulse| × lockPerImpulse`, up to `maxLockSeconds`): 0 → no roll, a shove → a short one, a strong blast → a long airborne one. The tumble's `minImpulse` threshold is gone; weapons tune it through blast power (blast-power D1). A back hit locks at least `hardLockSeconds`.
- **Roll exits (shared-roll D6):** a hit roll ends on the ground below `stopSpeed` once the lock ran out (`rollRestCount`); a jump once unlocked ends it (`launch` → `endRoll`); a dash roll ends when fire is let go; respawn and `maxSeconds` end any. The spin is sim state (`rollAngle` = ground speed / radius, kept in the air; D5) and turns back upright at `standUpSpeed` after the roll.
- Hooks for other systems: a burn or a knockout fling calls `reactToHit` with the impulse it gave (0 = no roll); `startRoll` is for rolls without a hit.
- Game feel stays render-only (game-feel D1): the helmet spring and flash read sim counters and the hit cues, never write `PlayerState`. The helmet is split from the body into its own ink + fill layers so it can move without breaking the one-line silhouette.
- Systems receive the slices they need, never the whole `GameState`, so features stay acyclic (game-engine-foundation D5).
- The weapon barrel is part of the helmet (owner, weapon-visuals-embellish D1–D8): one tapered barrel per weapon, never two, that starts at the helmet centre and pivots there through the full circle, in the helmet's flat cream with one shared outline, so its start never shows. It sits in the helmet's layers (rides the helmet spring) and is ranked over the eyes; the eyes do not move out of its way. Only the mouth differs per weapon (shared `weaponTube` geometry) and carries the weapon accent, which is the ready light: faded over a pale mouth while a shot recharges, full when ready. A shot (readiness dropping by more than half in a frame) pops the barrel short and fat (`feel.barrelPop`, `barrelPopScale`); one scale fits every mouth shape. There is no empty-weapon look, the barrel disappears at 0 ammo (weapon-visuals D3). The renderer takes `weapons` (item id + readiness) from the match; it never reads combat state.
