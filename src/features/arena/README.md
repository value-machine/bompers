# arena

## Purpose

The terrain runtime: turns a level's terrain shapes into a cell grid, answers collision queries, clears cells in blasts, and draws the terrain layer. The level model lives in `level`; which materials exist lives in `content-catalog`.

Seams for later work:

- **Blasts:** `features/combat` calls `clearCrater` (reach from the blast's power) in sim step 3 and turns `byLook` into destroy effect / sound events (catalog material) with the colour for debris.
- **Fire burn-out (fire-spread b1):** `burnOut(grid, samples)` empties burnt-out samples through `writeSample` (already-empty ones are skipped), then keeps the field true per chunk the samples fall in: `boundSolidsByNearestAir(grid, box, "all")` gives every neighbouring solid an edge distance and `remeasureAir` fixes the air (a crater bounds only `"unbreakable"` solids, since it writes exact rim distances itself). Returns `ClearedCells` like `clearCrater`. Cell slots carry the material's `burns` (fuel) and `douses` (fuel next to it never catches) for the fire feature. Since mover-materials lap 3 a cell slot carries the material's `reactions` (its row of the material × element table: what fire and cold do to it) instead of the `burns` / `douses` flags.
- **Things that spread through terrain (ice-element D4):** fire and ice run the shared sample front (`src/shared/utils/sampleFront.ts`) and the shared ray fan (`src/shared/utils/rayFan.ts`) on this grid; both read it as a plain `SampleField` (spacing, size, `cells`). A new element that travels through touching terrain uses those, not its own heap.
- **Frozen terrain (ice-element D5):** `TerrainGrid.frozen` is a flag per sample (`terrainGrid.ts`: `isFrozenSample`, `setSampleFrozen`, `thawAround`; `frozenVersion` bumps on every change). A frozen sample keeps its material but reads `frozenFriction` (the ice material's friction, set at import) in `groundFrictionBelow`, is skipped by `clearCrater`, and is bounded like unbreakable terrain next to a crater. A sample that is emptied loses the flag. The ice feature sets and clears it; the torch thaws it.
- **A part of the terrain drawn in the terrain's shape (owner, element-overlay-shape D1, 2026-11-18):** anything that covers some of the terrain's samples (burning ground, frozen ground, goo stains, a later element) is drawn as `terrainContour(grid, { grow, region: markedRegion(grid, samples) })`, never as its own blocks or rounded pieces. Where the part reaches the terrain's edge the contour is the terrain's edge (untouched slab corners, crater rims); inside the terrain its edge is a curve between marked and unmarked samples; a marked sample is always inside it and an unmarked solid one never is. Negative `grow` gives an inset copy (fire's hot inside). `TerrainGrid.shapeVersion` bumps on every sample write, so such a layer is redrawn only when its samples or the terrain change; `chunkVersions` / `boxShapeVersion` say the same per area, for a layer drawn in tiles (fire's body). The breakable clip is the same mechanism with `breakableRegion` (it replaced the contour's `breakableOnly` flag).
- **Outlines are lines (owner, v0.45.0, outline-system D1):** an outline of anything drawn from the field is `drawOutline(g, terrainRings(grid, options), …)`: the edge as closed rings, stroked at twice the visible width under the fill. Never a grown shape filled in ink under the fill: a fill that is late or missing then shows the whole shape in black (the flash seen while large chunks burnt). Terrain, moving parts and the fire's burning body use it; frost and stains have no ink. No caller passes a positive `grow` to `terrainContour` any more (it grew the old ink shapes and could flood the fire's box at a far zoom); only the fire's hot inside uses a negative one.
- **Touching shapes are one (owner, v0.45.0, outline-system D6–D7):** fills and lips are drawn from the field, never from each authored rect. Slabs of one colour that touch are one fill (the field's joins are square, slope-jump D3), and the lip is a band one sample high on every edge that faces up into the air (owner pick "band along the edge"): slab tops with their rounded corners, crater rims, burnt edges and thin platforms, and none on a pillar under a slab or under a pillar's foot. Two colours keep their own fill and lip; a colour under another colour has no lip there. A sharp polygon tip thinner than a sample is not drawn, as it never had an outline or collision. Still per authored piece: the ice gloss and inner rim.
- **New maps:** not here; they are `LevelDefinition`s (see `level`).

- `level` (`LevelDefinition`) and `content-catalog` (`resolveCatalogEntry` for material behaviour) through their `api/` files.
- `pixi.js` (renderer only), `src/shared/config/gamePalette.ts`, `src/shared/types/gameGeometry.types.ts`.
- Consumed by `player` (collision queries), `combat` (blasts, rocket collision), `collectibles` (weapon-crate landing, crate shadows) and `match` (state + render composition).

- `src/shared/utils/debugInk.ts`: the terrain and moving-part outline colours (`?debugInk=1` tints them; fire-black-flash diagnosis). With the flag on, `syncTerrainView` also runs `outlineRingIssue` on every rebuilt ring set and logs the first break as `[debugInk] … outline:` in the console (terrain-ink-spikes).

## Decisions

- Solid slabs are drawn from the level's piece shapes, not the grid: solid never changes, and a vector slab gives the spec's one continuous rounded shape; its edge may differ from collision by up to half a cell (visual-pass D2).
- Seam tiles are 2 × 2 cells (16 units at cell 8), so 16-unit plateaus still show the grid (visual-pass D3). Colours come from the slot's `colorId` (palette `terrainSwatches`).

- Terrain is a byte cell grid that blasts clear, not polygons or physics bodies (DOC_APP_VISION § Engine).
- A cell is breakable when its material is (no terrain roles; material-system D5). Every material draws as the same slab; breakable slabs are clipped by a stencil mask of the standing terrain (curved along crater rims, the authored shape elsewhere; terrain-curves D1, D2), and the only behaviour pattern is ice's gloss; breakable terrain is a plain solid slab like stone, told apart by colour and by how it reacts when hit (owner, terrain-curves D5, superseding the seam-grid cue of material-system D1).
- Out-of-range cells read as empty, so open map edges need no special case.
- `clearCrater` reports removed cells per material + colour so the one owner of "which cells a crater clears" also feeds destroy effects (combat D9).
- **Distance field (terrain-field D1–D7):** samples are 4 units apart (level `data-cell-size`, catalog default 4). Each sample has a slot and a signed distance to the nearest terrain edge (`distance`, positive inside, clamped to ± `FIELD_BAND_SAMPLES` = 5 samples, so it stays exact one body radius off the surface (slope-walking A5); read with `sampleDistance`); a sample is solid exactly while its distance is positive. Import seeds the distance from the level's shapes (`shapeSignedDistance`, union = max) and lifts interior samples to at least the bound from the nearest empty sample, so seams between touching shapes are not edges. `clearCrater({ x, y, reach, shape })` lowers each breakable sample to its distance from the rim `craterShapeDistance(shape) = reach ÷ toughness` (a true curve between samples), clears it at 0, bounds exposed unbreakable samples by the nearest empty one, and returns the touched `box`. `writeSample` is the only writer (slot + distance together). `anchored` marks unbreakable samples and the bottom row at load, for a later loose-parts lap (blast-power D20; all-breakable floating islands still need their own anchor rule there). Rect pieces seed with their drawn rounded corners (`SLAB_CORNER_RADIUS`), except a corner another piece carries on past (just beyond it along either edge is inside another shape: a pillar on a slab, a slab flush on a post), which stays square (`joinedCorners`, slope-jump D3, owner bug 2026-11-18: two rounded corners left a notch at the seam, the body circle sank into it and a jump up the wall snagged there). Touching slabs are one wall and floor in the field, so collision and the field-drawn ink read as one shape; the vector slab fills still round every corner (drawing is the outline-system lap). After a crater, air samples within the band are re-measured from their solid neighbours (`remeasureAir`, terrain-curves D9), so the outline leaves nothing where terrain is gone; cleared samples carry the exact rim distance, so the contour is exact along rims. Blob bodies collide on the distance with their round bottom (`terrainField.ts`, slope-walking); rect queries (`rectHitsSolid`: head box, crates) and friction still read the slots; the renderer draws crater rims from the distance (terrain-curves).
- Breakability is the material's toughness, not a flag (blast-power D1, D2): cardboard 0.7, earth 1, stone and ice `null` (unbreakable). The crater test is a distance rule per cell so the distance-field lap can reuse it with other crater shapes (blast-power D17–D19).
- **Moving parts (moving-parts D11, D16; lap B-1):** `TerrainGrid.movers` holds one `MoverBody` per moving part. Each body has a local field of its pieces in the rest pose, built by the same raster, and its pose, previous pose, pivot, origin, reach and friction. The pieces stay out of the static samples. `gridDistanceAt` reads static samples only (exported for the movers' squeeze test, which must not see the pushing part). `fieldDistanceAt`, `isSolidAt`, `rectHitsSolid`, `surfaceBelow` and `groundFrictionBelow` also see the parts in their current pose: a world point is mapped back to the rest pose, with a reach broadphase. That way every body collides with them without its own code. `moverDistanceAt`, `moverAt` and `moversDistanceAt` are the mover queries. Craters, fire, burn-out and the static outline never touch the parts. The renderer draws each part as its own container (the same slabs, plus an ink outline from its local field's contour, redrawn on an outline step), turned about the pivot and shifted by the pose each frame. There is no motion cue (owner, lap B B1). `terrainBodies(grid)` lists the fields a ray can meet, each placed in the world: the static terrain first, then every part in its pose this step (a `PosedField`, `src/shared/types/sampleFront.types.ts`). The torch, the freeze ray and the beam clash cast against it, so a beam ends on a part (mover-materials D4, lap 1); it is the start of the body list fire and ice will run on (mover-materials D1). **Bodies (mover-materials D1, lap 2a):** `bodyGrids(grid)` lists every body's own grid in the same order as `terrainBodies` (state kept per body, such as fire, is indexed by it) and `bodyWorldPoint(grid, body, local)` maps a point of a body's grid to the world. A part's field can now lose samples: fire burns a part of a burning material away through `burnOut` on the part's field, which keeps that field's distances true like the static one. The part's view follows its field's `shapeVersion`: the ink is redrawn and the breakable slabs are clipped by the breakable field still standing, as for static terrain. `createPoseLayer` / `placePoseLayer` are the one way a layer follows a part (the part's own view, goo stains, fire). Craters still never touch parts. **Cold on parts (lap 2b):** a part's field carries its own `frozen` flags. `groundFrictionBelow` reads the part samples under a span in the part's frame (`partFrictionBelow`), so a part is slippery exactly where it is frozen or made of ice; the one grip number per part (`MoverBody.friction`) is gone. `frozenVersionOf(grid)` is one number that changes when a frozen flag changes on any body (sound). **Bodies module (lap 4):** the body-list functions live in `services/terrainBodies.ts` (`terrainBodies`, `bodyGrids`, `bodyWorldPoint` and its inverse `bodyLocalPoint`, `bodyDistanceAt` / `bodyEdgeAbove` (one body's distance and its edge right above a world point, world-up whatever the body's turn: fire's flames use them so they rise world-up on a turned part), `frozenVersionOf`, `poolCleared`, `clearCraterOnBodies`); `terrainGrid.ts` keeps the functions that work on one grid. **Craters on parts (owner, mover-materials-l4 D1):** `clearCraterOnBodies(grid, crater)` cuts the static terrain and every moving part the blast reaches, each in its own grid where the part is this step (the crater's centre is mapped into the part's frame; crater shapes are round, so nothing else turns) and by its own materials' toughness: cardboard and earth parts crater, stone and ice parts and frozen patches stay whole. What was removed is pooled per material + colour (`ClearedBodies`). A part cut in two keeps moving as two pieces. This replaces "craters never touch the parts" above.
- `MoverBody.sounds` carries the level's author-declared part sounds (`move` and `end`, null = silent). Match plays them at the cue points of the part's cycle (`motionCuePoints`, `cuePointsPassed` in `src/shared/utils/levelMotion.ts`).
- The backdrop (sky, sun, parallax hills and clouds) moved out to the `backdrop` feature (mover-materials lap 4 preparation, 2026-11-18): it is a screen-space picture with no tie to the terrain grid, a second domain concept that had this feature's `services` layer at its file budget.
